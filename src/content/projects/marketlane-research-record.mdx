## Part 0. The project in one page

**What MarketLane Research is.** A production-style AI equity-research system for US public companies. It ingests SEC
10-K and 10-Q filings, parses and chunks them with heading and table structure, embeds them into PostgreSQL with
pgvector, serves retrieval, grounded-answer and filing-comparison endpoints from a FastAPI service behind a private
Next.js workspace, and keeps SEC CompanyFacts (XBRL) as the authority for reported numbers. Hard requirements: every
claim cited, every number traceable, say "insufficient evidence" instead of guessing, and ingestion that is idempotent
and safe under partial failure.

**Scale at archive time (24 October 2026).** One PostgreSQL 18.6 database of 1,058,558 rows: 110 companies, 3,783
filings (960 10-K, 2,823 10-Q), 61,778 sections, 522,539 tables, 283,982 chunks each with a 768-dimensional
`gemini-embedding-001` vector, 167,493 XBRL facts, 18,872 ingestion-stage records; 33.2 GB on disk; 15.2 GB of
content-addressed raw and normalized filing artifacts (7,564 objects). Served from Cloud Run with a global HTTPS load
balancer until the project was archived to S3 and deleted on 24 October 2026.

**What the work was actually about.** The question that organised the experimental programme (7 to 19 October 2026)
was not "which RAG technique is best" but "how do I know whether a change helped". The project's first obvious upgrade,
hybrid dense plus full-text retrieval with reciprocal rank fusion, made answers worse (judged correctness 0.610 to
0.531) because the lexical branch was issuer-blind: 82% of its top-20 chunks came from the wrong company. From that
point the programme was built around a measurement system that could tell a real gain from noise, label error, judge
error or planner luck: a frozen corpus, a source-audited benchmark, hash-pinned manifests, a retrieval-state contract,
pre-registered decision rules, paired statistics, a certified LLM judge, and a reserve set that has never been run.

**What was found.** On this corpus, which is 63% financial tables where the same table recurs in every filing of an
issuer with different numbers, every retrieval technique that rewards passages that *look* relevant lost to exact dense
retrieval plus a cheap issuer-aware selector. Hybrid FTS, BM25 fusion, SPLADE fusion, a cross-encoder reranker, MMR,
three deterministic selectors and a bounded agentic decomposition were all tested under pre-registered rules and
rejected. The bottleneck was located precisely: at dense depth 80, 41 of 149 development questions have complete
evidence in the candidate pool and lose it at final selection, and in 39 of those 41 the missing passage is a sibling
chunk of a filing the selector had already chosen. The remaining lever on retrieval is representation (chunks that know
which statement they belong to), not ranking.

**What was adopted.** Two changes, both cheap and deterministic, each cleared a pre-registered bar:

| Change | Evidence | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|
| E5A issuer-aware context selection over exact dense top-80 | Complete evidence@8 68/149 to 78/149, 10 gains / 0 regressions, exact McNemar p = 0.002; later judged 8/14 vs 5/14 on the paired rows | Experiment A (`A-20261010T062531Z-13111d4f`), J1 |
| Numeric route v3 (SEC XBRL facts, deterministic arithmetic, abstention guards) in front of the generator | Judged answer correctness on the 17 routed rows 16/17 vs 9/17, 8 gains / 1 loss, p = 0.039; about +4.7 points on the 149 eligible development rows by construction | Experiment K2 (`k2_numeric_route_pipeline_v1.json`, freeze `4366a00`) |

The selected pipeline configuration is `marketlane-pipeline-v2-dense-e5a-numeric-route` (hash `edb0f62c…`), with the
previous version's hash recorded inside it and a parity check reproducing the evaluation contexts 160 of 160. A separate
benchmark of the *served* production route (ticker-filtered hybrid plus hosted reranker) on 9 October found no clear
difference against E5A, because the UI's company selection already removes off-issuer passages; production was left
unchanged pending the G4 wiring step.

**What the judge work found.** The LLM judge failed its conformance suite on 10 October (6 of 13 cases). It was rebuilt
so the model reports findings and code makes verdicts, with the evidence call never seeing the answer. Version 5.4
passes a 19-case live suite 19/19 and reaches κ 0.95 on answers and 0.68 on evidence against owner-confirmed labels. The
honest residual: twelve evidence false accepts remain because the model fills in the period and value the obligation
asks for even when the passage does not contain them. A self-reported field is a claim, not a verification.

**Where the platform is now.** On 20 October the decision was taken to move infrastructure from GCP to AWS and LLM
inference from Vertex Gemini to the Claude API. On 24 October everything needed to rebuild was archived to private S3
storage (about 44 GB, Glacier Instant Retrieval, about 0.25 USD per month; eval backups under Object Lock legal hold), every copy verified against its GCP original, the database dump test-restored with zero errors, and the GCP
project deleted. The AWS rebuild is a written plan with phases and gates; none of it has been executed.

**Cost of knowing.** Total model spend across the programme is about 40 USD. Every live run had a hard call and dollar
cap enforced from returned token counts. Six of the thirteen dev160 experiments cost 0 USD in model calls. The archive
cost about 1.35 USD one-off; the GCP project's standing burn before deletion was about 36 USD per month.

## Part 1. The system: product, architecture, infrastructure, operations

### 1.1 The product

MarketLane Research answers an individual researcher's questions about US public companies from primary sources.
Sources are SEC 10-K and 10-Q filing text for narrative and SEC CompanyFacts (XBRL) for reported numbers. Current user
workflows: search filing passages, generate a grounded answer with validated citations,
compare the latest and previous 10-K or 10-Q by topic, and use internal services for structured fact lookup and
deterministic calculations. Non-goals: trading advice, news or social feeds, earnings-call transcripts, live market
data, GraphRAG, a multi-agent runtime, distributed ingestion queues, a public fact-lookup endpoint.

### 1.3 Data model and durability

A migration tool owns the schema. Company identity is CIK; filing identity is `(company_id, accession_number)`. Chunks carry
filing metadata, section and heading path, source text, a content hash, optional parent context, and a 768-dimensional
vector with model and version provenance. Documents track `pending`, `complete` or `failed`; retrieval joins only
complete documents, so a refresh that fails leaves the last published representation searchable.

Raw production filings are immutable SHA-256-addressed GCS objects keyed by company, accession and content hash, written
create-only; normalized JSON, keyed additionally by parser version, is a rebuildable cache. Publication prepares everything outside the transaction, then commits sections, tables, chunks,
embeddings and metadata together. Per-document PostgreSQL advisory claims prevent two workers publishing the same
accession. Hashes and parser, chunker and embedding versions let reruns skip unchanged work and reuse compatible vectors.

### 1.4 The two retrieval paths

| | Served path (`/v1/retrieve`) | Evaluated path (pipeline config v2) |
|---|---|---|
| Scoping | SQL ticker filter from the UI's company selection, query expansion, latest-filing pin | Issuer resolved from the question text and a metadata catalog (E5A) |
| Candidates | Dense (HNSW) + PostgreSQL FTS, pool 100, RRF k = 60 | Exact-eligibility dense to depth 80 (eligibility before ranking; chunk-UUID tie-break) |
| Ranking | Google `semantic-ranker-default-004`, top-10, dense anchor kept | E5A deterministic selection to 8 passages (about 2 ms) |
| Numbers | Generator reads them from passages | Numeric route v3 answers single-metric, change, ratio and CAGR questions from XBRL before generation, or abstains |
| Generation | `gemini-3.1-pro-preview` (global endpoint, temperature 1.0, thinking HIGH, 16k output) since 9 October | Evaluated with `gemini-2.5-flash` (temperature 0, thinking budget 0) so replays stay faithful |

Closing the gap between these two paths is the open G4 step (Part 13). The 9 October production benchmark (Part 5
§5.9) explains why the served path was not changed immediately: its ticker filter already removes off-issuer passages.

### 1.8 Why financial retrieval is not ordinary retrieval

A question such as *"Calculate the percentage change in operating income from FY2018 to FY2020"* needs the right
company, consolidated operating income for both fiscal years, consistent accounting basis, correct units, and a valid
calculation. A dense retriever can return eight passages about operating income and miss the requested year, or rank
another company's statement highly because the table vocabulary is identical. The programme therefore separates
candidate availability, ranking and selection, complete evidence, answer correctness, and, as a first-class engineering
object, evaluator correctness.

## Part 3. Methodology: the experimental discipline

### 3.1 Freeze before you look

- Frozen inputs, pinned by hash: corpus, dataset release (`dev_baseline_v1`, release `b7610317…`), dense candidate pool
  (`dense_exact_e2_depth120`, manifest `fd2ae76a…`), selector code (E5A freeze `26ccd243…`), issuer catalog and metric
  vocabulary, judge rubric, CompanyFacts store (`bdfe8d37…`). The runner refuses to start if any pin drifts.
- Pre-registration before the first output exists: a written pre-registration (hypothesis, arms, endpoints, decision
  rule, statistics, what counts as invalid) and a config whose SHA-256 is bound in an approval record with the
  pre-registration's, the runner's and the pinned modules' hashes. Nothing is tuned after a result is seen.
- Feasibility probes are allowed and disclosed: a probe over runtime rows that reads no gold and scores nothing may
  precede the freeze; any constant changed after a probe is named.
- Reports are created exclusively, never overwritten. Invalid and interrupted runs are kept beside valid ones.

### 3.2 One change, same everything else

One capability per experiment, the parent run recorded, the same logical retrieval state, the eight-passage budget
fixed. The reference arm must reproduce Experiment A's E0_v2 and E5A contexts on 160/160 rows and the E5A replay
receipt must read FAITHFUL, or the run is invalid.

### 3.3 Gold never reaches the runtime

Retrieval, selection, routing and generation receive only `{query_id, question}`. Reference answers, obligations, hop
plans, chunk hints, issuer labels and fact periods are evaluator-side only.

### 3.4 Statistics that respect the sample

Exact McNemar on discordant rows plus a 10,000-resample cluster bootstrap over leakage groups (seed 20261009), Holm
correction across families. Gains and regressions reported by row ID, question type and cohort. Decision rules name
minimum gains, maximum regressions, a p threshold and "do not fall" clauses (typical: gains ≥ 5, regressions ≤ 1,
Holm p < 0.05, macro recall not lower, native complete@8 not lower). An n = 14 result with p = 0.25 is reported as a
direction, not an effect size.

### 3.5 Labels are proxies, and the proxy is named

Retrieval-only results carry the label *development, deterministic chunk-hint evidence proxy*. Judged results carry
the amendment A1 disclosure. Every number is tagged development; the 32 reserved rows have never been loaded.

### 3.6 The evaluator is tested like code

Offline conformance with an oracle provider (must pass) and an adversarial accept-all provider (must fail in the
expected way) before any live call. Live suites with repeats; any infrastructure failure invalidates the whole suite,
which is rerun whole. A judge version is certified only when the suite passes and κ clears 0.6 under a rule fixed
before the run; the runner compares the certification record's decision field literally. A judge-independent check
(the deterministic numeric scorer) is kept beside the judge.

### 3.7 Money and failure policy

Every live run has a call cap and a USD cap enforced from returned token counts (`MeteredProvider`). Unscored rows stay
unscored; no per-row retry after a live call. Cloud SQL was started only when a run needed it and stopped after.

### 3.8 Implementation and review

Implementation was separated from independent artifact review: every pre-registration, benchmark release, judge
certification and result report was reviewed by someone other than its author before it counted, and the decisions
that define the benchmark (semantics, reporting basis, taxonomy, exposure exceptions, release approval, label
confirmation) were taken by one accountable person and recorded.

## Part 4. Pre-history: the legacy benchmark lineage and why it was abandoned

Before the programme, evaluation grew through six benchmark versions on `eval-corpus-v0.1` (corpus hash `c7e4040a…`):

| Version | Content | What it taught |
|---|---|---|
| v0.1 | Snapshot builder; silver suite with 0 validated cases, no XBRL mappings | Provenance rules written before data existed |
| v0.2 | FinanceBench-native 46 cases; marketlane silver 15,936 generated cases; XBRL company-facts and evidence mappings | Scale from templates is cheap and mostly untrustworthy |
| v0.3 | Adversarial additions, immutable sources, audit v0.3 | Benchmark edits need an audit trail |
| v0.5 screened | 59-case evidence-screened cohort | A screened cohort is not an adjudicated benchmark |
| v0.5 audited | 216 cases; 33 annotation modifications; 4 ambiguous; dense/lexical/hybrid/reranker baselines | Reported answerable recall@10 49.1%, gold-chunk recall@10 39.9%, complete evidence@10 37.5%, multi-hop 4.7%, answer correctness 61.2%, latency p50/p95 10.32 s / 20.96 s |
| v0.6 evidence | Evidence re-adjudication; reranker complete evidence@20 70.75% (150/212) after label correction, with no system change | Label changes move scores as much as system changes |

Investigating failures on this lineage found that an exact-period filter had removed 69 gold items, that truncating an
RRF top-100 to 20 for reranking lost 60 relevant items, that an audit of 18 apparent reranking failures found 17
annotation problems and one genuine rank drop, and that a reconstruction of 61 failure cases could restore only 189 of
1,039 unique top-20 passages, 682 of 4,604 pool passages and 13 of 61 reranker orderings. Legacy XBRL-silver questions
labelled "fiscal year" often held Q4 three-month values; 10-Q operating cash flow questions held year-to-date values.
**Decision:** stop treating the lineage as ground truth; keep historical assets as candidate material; rebuild
source-verifiable questions against a frozen corpus; refuse to extrapolate from a partially restorable run. The v1
development benchmark (an immutable copy of the audited v0.5 cohort) and the Benchmarks page registry in the web app
preserve the history without rewriting it.

Two cohorts bridged the lineage to the programme: Experimental-50 v1 (50 cases with semantic obligations) and the

## Part 5. Phase 0: making the measurement trustworthy, and the first results (Experimental-50 v3)

### 5.1 Challenge A: the benchmark can be wrong when the code is right

Historical annotations pointed to particular chunks when equivalent evidence existed elsewhere; some fact dates were
copied from the filing date; original and restated values differed. The fix separated the period of the requested fact
from the reporting period of the source document, kept chunk references as non-mandatory hints, and allowed directly
stated, deterministically derived and carefully supported inferred evidence. Labels were not rewritten in response to
scores.

### 5.2 Challenge B: seven technical failures distorted the baseline

The first dense run scored only 43 of 50 answer cases; the failures were citation-format and judge-schema problems
(grouped citations like `[1, 3]`, a terminal-punctuation gate, an unenforced judge schema). The adapter was hardened
(69 focused tests), retrieval-state hardening passed 92. The canonical rerun scored 50/50 with zero operational failures
and identical rankings; the mean moved 0.615 on 43 to 0.610 on 50, a measurement-reliability change.

### 5.3 Challenge C: "same inputs" did not give the same retrieval

A request for 20 candidates returned 16. The database held about 284,000 rows but the evaluation was restricted to
28,452 frozen chunks. On fresh connections PostgreSQL used an approximate HNSW path (`hnsw.ef_search = 40`, iterative
scan off) that found a small set over the whole table *before* the frozen-corpus filter removed out-of-scope rows; from
the sixth execution of the prepared statement the generic plan returned full, differently ordered results.

```text
Before (planner-dependent):  ANN over ~284k rows → small set → frozen filter → sometimes < 20 results
After (explicit semantics):  all 28,452 eligible vectors → exact cosine → ordered top-k, chunk-UUID tie-break
```

An evaluation-only `FrozenExactDenseRetriever` (production retrieval untouched) applies eligibility before ranking,
checks the full vector count, uses exact cosine distance and breaks float64 ties by chunk UUID. Verification repeated
representative queries 21 times each across fresh connections, executions, orders and plan modes. The new E0_v2
baseline (`arch-20261007T180013128330Z-638888b8`) returned 20 candidates for every query; the depth-120 diagnostic
reproduced its top-20 prefix 61/61. Retrieval p50 rose from about 1.41 s to 1.64 s. 58 of 61 lists were identical; the
three changes were recorded as infrastructure corrections. A dense execution policy (v2, then v3 with configurable
depth and `DensePolicyGuardV3`) pins model, semantics and session settings.

### 5.4 Challenge D: reproducibility needs an execution contract

The original transaction snapshot could not be reopened. It was replaced by a versioned retrieval-state contract:
corpus and vector fingerprints, embedding identity, index and session settings, candidate ordering, configuration and
code provenance, mandatory preflight and postflight verification, a guarded read-only repeatable-read phase, and a
read-only heartbeat after an early run lost its transaction to an idle Cloud SQL proxy. Runs persist candidates,
scores, contexts, generation outputs, failures, per-query metrics and latency.

### 6.1 Isolation and supply

### 6.2 The strict multi-hop rule

H1 must produce an intermediate result that materially determines which distinct H2 evidence target is needed, and H2
must require a genuinely separate evidence or reasoning unit. Another row of the same table, another bullet, or extra
arithmetic is not a hop. Authors first labelled 79 questions multi-hop; adversarial review found 11 clearly strict.

### 6.3 Tiers, selection and the audit funnel

3,162 inputs → 991 exclusions → master pool 2,171 (Tier A 428 → 445, Tier B 1,517, Tier C 209). The
150-question ceiling was removed. The architecture-blind selector proposed 190 (185 Tier A, 5 Tier B) across 28 issuers, 21
reasoning archetypes, 155 template families, 30 strict multi-hop.

| Stage | Expansion count | Main outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| Architecture-blind proposal | 190 | Initial diverse selection |
| Financial source validation (eight issuer-disjoint reviewer batches) | 186 | Three same-fact duplicates removed; one Chubb equity question held (adjusted $51,268M vs reported $51,667M unspecified) |
| Evidence-obligation construction | 182 | Two Boeing BDS cases excluded (table identity by totals and ordering only), an American Express write-off table excluded (unheaded), an American Tower quarter-driver question held |
| Comprehensive audit | 182 | 80 blocking findings affecting 58 questions |
| Remediation v2 / v3 / v4 | 147 / 141 / 134 | Source identities, units, obligations corrected; unsupported sources excluded |
| Final development snapshot | **131** | Three high-risk questions quarantined |

Strict multi-hop fell 40 → 30 → 28 → 26 → **8**. Three recurring problems defined the standard: source identity (a
number matching a labelled figure elsewhere does not establish which entity an unheaded table describes), unit scale
(the corpus lost many "in thousands / in millions" captions; dimensionless ratios survive, dollar amounts need explicit
scale evidence), and accounting period (reporting period is not fact period; YTD vs standalone quarter; as-filed vs
restated).

### 6.4 Answer obligations versus evidence obligations

At the 182-row stage: 524 answer-required and 825 evidence-required obligations (direct / derived / inference
635 / 189 / 1), 1,490 verified source quotes, 152 alternative evidence groups across 75 questions, 79 deterministic
derivations recalculated with zero mismatches. A ratio needs one reported number in the answer but authoritative
evidence for numerator, denominator, time basis and formula. Semantically equivalent evidence satisfies an obligation;
historical chunk IDs are provenance hints.

### 6.5 The evaluator became an engineering problem

An obligation written so two dates' values could be chosen independently let an invalid cross-measure pair score full
credit; the contract was rewritten atomically. The runtime scorer's Boolean format did not prove the restriction was
enforced, so the question was quarantined. Mutation tests found validators that accepted a `SUPPORTED` record without a
valid source binding. Three follow-ups: FU-1 (loader isolation and scorer conformance), FU-2 (financial identity and
unit validation), FU-3 (structure-aware parsing as a separately versioned corpus).

### 6.6 The frozen release, the reserve and the backup

The frozen release `dev_baseline_v1`, labelled `SOURCE_AUDITED_DEVELOPMENT_BASELINE`: 192 questions (131 expansion, 61 seed), 8
strict multi-hop, 126 provisionally fresh, 22/22 integrity checks. Not a certified holdout. The reserve allocation
(approved 9 October) withholds 32 provisionally fresh rows; the loader drops them before adaptation and every run folder
is scanned for reserved IDs, question text or hashes. The 160 development rows contain 149 semantic rows and 11 native
exact-scored rows. The snapshot, allocation and frozen corpus were backed up to a GCS bucket under temporary holds (23 files,
SHA256SUMS verified, restored and re-validated 20/20), and on 24 October copied to an S3 Object Lock bucket under legal
hold (Part 12).

## Part 14. The root-cause map

```text
                        149 semantic development questions, dense depth 80
                                            |
            +-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
            |                               |                               |
   78 complete in final 8          41 complete in pool,            30 not complete
   (E5A, 52.3%)                    lost at selection                in the pool
            |                               |                               |
   generation errors:              39/41: missing chunk is a        per-obligation sub-queries
   wrong line read, wrong          sibling of a chosen filing;      add +1 (E8); lexical union
   sign, mis-computed CAGR,        36/41 chunks have no heading;    adds +2 to pool, 0 to context
   declines with figures in        regex detectors miss gold        (G1b); the lever is
   XBRL → numeric route (K2)       captions and fire on footers     representation (FU-3 proper)
   fixes 7 net on routed rows      (G1′); statement typing +1 (H1);
                                   19 rows gated by metric
                                   extraction before any rule
```

Why every ranker lost: the corpus is near-duplicate tables that differ by period. Topical relevance (dense similarity,
cross-encoder score, SPLADE expansion, BM25 terms, sub-query top hits) finds the right company and the right table three
quarters of the time and cannot tell this year's instance from last year's. The period signal is carried by the
whole-chunk dense embedding better than by any bag of terms. The selection signal that is missing is *which statement is
this chunk*, and the frozen corpus dropped it.

Why the served path did not lose to E5A: the UI's ticker filter does in SQL what E5A does from question text, so the
production route already had zero off-issuer passages; the two paths fail on different rows.

Why the numeric route won: on the rows it touches, the text pipeline's errors were reading and arithmetic errors on
passages that contained the right figures, plus declines on figures present in XBRL. A parser and a division do not make
those errors, and they cite the accession.

Why the judge needed rebuilding twice: rules the rubric stated were not applied by the model; moving them into code on
reliably reported findings (measure, units) fixed them; moving them onto findings the model invents when the passage
lacks the figure (period, value) did not. The next rule checks the report against the source text.

## Part 15. What is established, and what may not be claimed

**Established, as development evidence on the frozen 28-company corpus:**

- Exact dense retrieval plus E5A issuer-aware selection delivers complete evidence@8 of 78/149 against 68/149 for dense
  top-8, with zero paired regressions, and the same selector wins the judged paired answer comparison 8/14 vs 5/14.
- Dense retrieval makes complete evidence available for 119 of 149 questions within 80 candidates; 41 lose it at
  selection; in 39 of those the missing chunk is a sibling of a filing already chosen; 36 of those chunks have no heading.
- Equal-weight RRF with any lexical arm tested (FTS, BM25, SPLADE), a BGE cross-encoder at 512 or 1024 tokens, MMR,
  three deterministic selectors and a bounded agentic decomposition all fail their pre-registered rules on this corpus,
  each with an inspectable mechanism.
- The served ticker-filtered hybrid route and E5A are not distinguishable on the 50 answer cases; both beat unscoped
  dense.
- A deterministic XBRL route answers the numeric questions it parses more correctly than the text pipeline, 16/17 vs
  9/17 on the rows it touches under a certified judge, and abstains on the rest.
- The LLM judge v5.4 passes a 19-case live suite and reaches κ 0.95 / 0.68 against owner-confirmed labels; the judged
  baseline for the text pipeline is 24/50.
- The evaluation infrastructure: frozen corpus and benchmark with hashes, retrieval-state contract, 61/61 and 160/160
  identity checks, reserve isolation, pre-registration binding, cost caps, exclusive-create reports.
- The platform: idempotent content-addressed ingestion with atomic publish and advisory claims; a database of 1.06M rows

## Part 17. Consolidated lessons

**On measurement**

1. Build the measuring instrument before the system it measures. A retrieval failure report had become a label-quality
   report (17 of 18 "failures" were annotations).
2. A reproducible benchmark is a systems artifact. A corpus hash is not enough if the planner can change which
   candidates survive.
3. Separate the funnel: candidate availability, selection, complete evidence, answer correctness, evaluator correctness.
4. Read per-query diffs, not means. A mean hid that rank 1 was decided by a string sort.
5. Small benchmarks are fine if you report their resolution honestly: one exact-gold case is 9.1 points; n = 14 is a
   direction.
6. Benchmark the thing you ship before you replace it. The served route had already solved the issuer problem a
   different way.

**On retrieval in this domain**

7. In a multi-entity corpus, scoping is retrieval; ranking only orders what scoping allows.
8. A larger candidate pool is an upper bound, not a gain.
9. Semantic diversity is not evidence completeness.
10. Equal-weight rank fusion is only as good as its weaker input.
11. Topical relevance does not carry the fiscal period. Reranker, SPLADE and sub-query top hits all found the right
    table and the wrong year.
12. Measure which axis the loss is on before building a selector for it. One query (does the missing chunk share a
    document with a chosen chunk?) settled a line of three experiments.
13. Measure the detector before the selector.
14. Enrich before you re-parse. A twelve-second sidecar kept every paired comparison alive.
15. Ask the pool question before the budget question. "Agentic" is a hypothesis with a cost, not a tier.
16. Closing a line is a result.

**On numbers and generation**

17. Numbers belong to structured data; text retrieval should cite them, not compute them.
18. Abstention is the product. Two of the five K2 fixes were refusals.
19. Read your component's outputs before you pay to judge them. Four losses were visible for free and became a
    specification.
20. Better retrieval reduces evidence failures; it cannot make the generator use the evidence correctly.

**On judges**

21. A rubric is a wish; a rule is a guarantee.
22. Grade the evidence without the answer.
23. A reported field is a claim. Verify the finding against the source text.
24. Conformance suites must be allowed to fail you. Rerun whole, never cherry-pick.
25. Find a check that is not the judge.
26. Say what a new judge version should move before it runs.
27. Carry the disclosure with the number, not in a footnote.

**On platform and process**

28. Design the data model for recovery: content-addressed artifacts and a logical dump restored with zero errors made
    the decommission a one-day job instead of a risk.
29. A provider swap is an experiment. Certification, frozen vectors and baselines are properties of model plus rubric
    plus config, and none of them transfer.
30. Pre-registration is cheap insurance. When the result arrives there is nothing to tune and nothing to argue about.
31. Cap the spend before the first call. About 40 USD bought every number in this document.
32. Make the runner refuse. A literal-string certification check kept a "CERTIFIED (pending)" from ever scoring.
33. Keep every run, including the invalid ones.
34. Deliberately not building something ("I measured it and it didn't earn its place") is a stronger answer than
    adding it.

## Part 18. Infrastructure decisions and trade-offs, in one table

| Decision | Choice made | Why | What would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| One database for metadata, text, vectors and facts | PostgreSQL + pgvector + GIN FTS | Co-located filters, joins and atomic publication; no second store to keep consistent | Measured vector-query or index-maintenance load competing with publication at scale |
| Exact vs approximate dense retrieval | HNSW online; exact-eligibility retriever for evaluation | ANN meets selective filters badly (the planner bug); evaluation must be a pure function of the query | Recall checks and issuer-scoped partitions at two orders of magnitude more chunks |
| Hybrid + reranker online vs dense + selector evaluated | Served path unchanged pending G4 | Benchmark found no clear difference; the ticker filter already removes off-issuer passages | API wiring, parity through HTTP, a load test of both paths |
| Retrieval and answer as separate endpoints | Evidence-only `/v1/retrieve`, evidence-constrained `/v1/research` | The UI reviews evidence; server-side citation validation | A composed `/v1/ask` that carries the pipeline config hash |
| Ingestion coordination | One Cloud Run task, ≤ 2 in-process workers, advisory locks | Prevents duplicate publication without a queue or coordinator | Measured 100-issuer throughput that exceeds one task's window |
| Scale-to-zero containers | Cloud Run | Research footprint; zero cost when idle | Fargate has no native scale-to-zero; the plan budgets for always-on tasks or a scheduled scale-down |
| Observability backend | Vendor-neutral OTel behind a facade; Prometheus and Phoenix locally; Cloud Logging in production | Instrumentation survives a cloud move; nothing can fail a request | Choosing a hosted trace backend after cutover |
| Cost policy | Start the database only for runs; cap every live model run; archive to Glacier Instant Retrieval | A fixed research budget | A provider spend limit agreed before Phase 4 |

## Part 20. Handover

Reading order for a new engineer: Parts 0 to 3 and Part 12 of this record; the architecture, decisions, failure-mode
and ingestion rollout documents; the observability architecture, tracing, metrics and runbook documents; the experiment
pre-registrations and their result reports, with Experiments D, F and K2 as templates; the judge conformance analysis
and certification records; the production benchmark report and errata; the migration plan and the archive record.

Operating rules to hand over verbatim: branch per experiment; restore the evaluated generator on the experiment branch
only; pre-register and bind an approval before the first output; never load the reserve; gold never reaches the
runtime; one change per experiment; exact McNemar plus cluster bootstrap; label every number; keep every run; a
provider swap is an experiment; do not release the eval-backup holds without written approval.

Costs to budget by: a judge conformance suite about 2.9 USD; a 40-row packet re-score about 2.7 USD; a 78-row judged
re-score about 5.5 USD; a K-style paired judged experiment on about 20 rows about 2.5 USD; retrieval-only experiments
0 USD; a full archive restore about 0.75 USD of Glacier retrieval.
