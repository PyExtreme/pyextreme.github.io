# MarketLane AI

**AI-native market intelligence for equity research, technical analysis, opportunity discovery, and decision support.**

**Live Product:** [staging.marketlaneai.com](https://staging.marketlaneai.com)

---

## Overview

MarketLane AI is an AI-powered market intelligence platform I designed and built to explore how modern AI systems can improve the way investors and traders discover, analyze, and monitor opportunities in public markets.

The core idea is simple:

> Financial markets produce far more information than a human can continuously process. MarketLane turns market data, technical signals, company context, and AI reasoning into a structured decision-support system.

Instead of building another stock screener or wrapping an LLM around financial data, I designed MarketLane as an **AI-native research and market intelligence system**.

The platform brings together:

* market-data pipelines
* quantitative screening
* technical-analysis engines
* AI-assisted market interpretation
* stock-level intelligence
* signal detection
* alerting
* structured research workflows
* portfolio/watchlist context
* LLM-powered reasoning

into a unified product.

---

# Why I Built It

Most investing tools fall into one of two categories.

Traditional screeners are excellent at filtering structured data but provide little interpretation.

AI assistants are good at language and reasoning, but frequently lack reliable market context, deterministic calculations, and structured workflows.

MarketLane explores the architecture between these two worlds.

```text
Traditional Market Data
        ↓
Quantitative / Technical Analysis
        ↓
Signal & Opportunity Detection
        ↓
Context Construction
        ↓
AI Reasoning
        ↓
Investor Decision Support
```

The objective is not for an LLM to predict the market.

The objective is to build an intelligence layer capable of answering:

```text
What is happening?

Why is it interesting?

What changed?

Which stocks deserve attention?

What evidence supports the signal?

What should I investigate next?
```

# 1. Market Data Pipeline

Market intelligence starts with reliable data.

The platform is designed around an ingestion and transformation pipeline similar to:

```text
External Market Data
        ↓
Ingestion
        ↓
Validation / Normalization
        ↓
Historical Storage
        ↓
Feature Computation
        ↓
Market Intelligence APIs
        ↓
Product + AI Layer
```

Rather than asking an LLM to derive quantitative information from raw text, MarketLane computes market features programmatically.

Examples include:

* price returns
* moving averages
* relative volume
* volatility
* momentum
* trend structure
* breakout conditions
* support/resistance context
* price-relative indicators
* market regime information

This creates a clean separation between:

```text
Deterministic computation
            vs
Probabilistic reasoning
```

That separation is one of the most important architectural principles in the system.

---

# 2. Screening Engine

A major part of the platform is an extensible screening layer.

Instead of implementing screeners directly inside individual UI pages, the system models screening as a reusable computation layer.

Conceptually:

```text
Universe
   ↓
Market Features
   ↓
Conditions / Rules
   ↓
Ranking
   ↓
Candidate Stocks
   ↓
Context Enrichment
```

This makes it possible to build screens around combinations of:

* momentum
* price trend
* volume
* volatility
* breakouts
* moving-average structures
* relative performance
* technical setups
* fundamental constraints

The broader engineering idea is to treat a screener as a **query over derived market state**, rather than a collection of hard-coded pages.

---

# 3. Signal Detection

MarketLane separates **data** from **signals**.

A price increase is data.

A price increase accompanied by abnormal volume, a resistance breakout, improving momentum, and sector strength may constitute a signal worth investigating.

The signal layer therefore operates over derived market features.

```text
Raw observations
      ↓
Derived features
      ↓
Signal conditions
      ↓
Signal confidence/context
      ↓
User-facing opportunity
```

This architecture allows MarketLane to evolve from simple technical screens toward richer multi-factor intelligence without tightly coupling those rules to the frontend.

---

# 4. AI Intelligence Layer

The AI layer converts structured market information into something a human can quickly reason about.

MarketLane uses **Vertex AI for model inference**, while the application itself is deployed separately on AWS.

This resulted in a deliberately cloud-agnostic model boundary:

```text
MarketLane Application
        │
        │ structured context
        ▼
Model Gateway / AI Layer
        │
        ▼
Vertex AI
        │
        ▼
Structured AI Response
        │
        ▼
Validation / Rendering
```

Instead of simply submitting:

```text
"Analyze AAPL"
```

the system can construct a richer context containing information such as:

```text
symbol
price context
recent returns
trend
volume
volatility
technical indicators
identified signals
relevant historical context
```

The LLM becomes a **reasoning and interpretation layer on top of deterministic analytics**, rather than being responsible for performing every calculation itself.

---

# 5. AI Output Design

Free-form LLM responses quickly become difficult to use in a product.

MarketLane therefore treats AI responses as structured application data.

Conceptually:

```json
{
  "summary": "...",
  "market_structure": "...",
  "bullish_signals": [],
  "bearish_signals": [],
  "risks": [],
  "important_levels": [],
  "observations": []
}
```

Structured generation provides several advantages:

* predictable rendering
* easier validation
* safer downstream processing
* reusable components
* consistent user experience
* simpler evaluation
* easier model replacement

It also creates a clear contract between the AI layer and the product layer.

---

# 6. AI Is Not the Calculation Engine

One architectural principle I intentionally adopted is:

> Never use an LLM for calculations that deterministic software can perform reliably.

For example:

```text
Moving average calculation → deterministic code

Volume percentile → deterministic code

Price return → deterministic code

Signal detection → rules / analytics

Signal interpretation → LLM

Natural-language explanation → LLM
```

This reduces hallucination risk while making the system significantly easier to debug.

It also lets individual components evolve independently.

# 7. Stock Intelligence

At the stock level, the goal is to build a consolidated intelligence surface rather than force users to jump between disconnected tools.

A stock can conceptually be represented as:

```text
Stock
│
├── Price / Chart
├── Market Structure
├── Technical Indicators
├── Momentum
├── Volume
├── Signals
├── AI Interpretation
├── Watchlist Context
└── Research
```

The UI is designed so that quantitative information remains visible while AI provides an additional reasoning layer.

That distinction matters.

The AI should explain the market state—not hide it.

# 8. Market Intelligence Architecture

The longer-term architecture treats individual observations as part of a market-event system.

```text
Market Data
    ↓
Feature Computation
    ↓
Signal Detection
    ↓
Market Events
    ↓
Ranking / Prioritization
    ↓
AI Interpretation
    ↓
Alerts / Dashboard / Research
```

An event may represent something such as:

```text
breakout_detected
volume_anomaly
momentum_acceleration
trend_reversal
support_break
resistance_break
technical_setup_detected
```

This event-driven model allows the same underlying market intelligence to power multiple product experiences.

For example:

```text
signal
  ├── dashboard
  ├── stock page
  ├── alert
  ├── daily market scan
  └── AI analysis
```

instead of implementing independent logic for every feature.

# 9. Designing for Scale

Although MarketLane started as a founder-built product, I designed its architecture around boundaries that could support a much larger system.

The core separation is:

```text
Data ingestion

Feature computation

Signal engine

AI intelligence

API layer

Product UI
```

This means computationally expensive workloads can eventually move independently into asynchronous or distributed workers without forcing a complete rewrite of the product.

For example:

```text
             ┌── Real-time APIs
             │
Market Data ─┼── Scheduled Analytics
             │
             └── Async Signal Processing
                       │
                       ▼
                Intelligence Store
                       │
              ┌────────┴────────┐
              │                 │
             API            AI Layer
```

The architecture is intended to support future workloads such as:

* larger stock universes
* intraday market scans
* scheduled intelligence jobs
* event-triggered alerts
* asynchronous AI analysis
* portfolio-level reasoning

without coupling them directly to user requests.

# 10. Caching and Cost-Aware AI

Financial applications repeatedly request overlapping information.

For example:

```text
AAPL technical context
market regime
sector context
latest derived features
```

may be reused across several requests.

A production architecture therefore benefits from separating:

```text
slow-changing context
fast-changing market context
user-specific context
```

This creates opportunities for:

* application caching
* precomputed market features
* context reuse
* prompt caching
* reduced model input
* asynchronous pre-generation

The goal is to avoid treating every AI request as an entirely independent workload.

# 11. Reliability

An AI financial product cannot treat:

```text
HTTP 200
```

as equivalent to:

```text
correct intelligence generated
```

There are several independent failure modes:

```text
data unavailable

stale market data

feature computation failure

AI provider failure

invalid structured response

partial output

timeout

rate limit

frontend rendering failure
```

A stronger reliability model therefore separates:

```text
request success

data validity

AI generation success

schema validity

product delivery
```

This is an architectural pattern I have become particularly interested in while building production AI systems.

# 13. Evaluation

One of the harder problems in an AI market-intelligence system is determining whether the generated analysis is actually useful.

I think about evaluation across multiple layers.

### Deterministic correctness

Can the system correctly calculate:

* indicators
* price changes
* screening conditions
* signals?

These should be unit-testable.

### Grounding

Does the AI response remain consistent with the structured market context provided to it?

### Structured-output reliability

Does generation conform to the expected schema?

### Consistency

Do semantically equivalent queries produce materially consistent conclusions?

### Product usefulness

Does the generated analysis help users identify important information faster than inspecting the raw data themselves?

Separating these evaluation categories prevents vague measures such as "the AI answer looks good" from becoming the quality standard.

# 14. Security Boundaries

External model APIs are treated as dependencies rather than trusted internal components.

Important boundaries include:

```text
User Input
   ↓
Input validation
   ↓
Context construction
   ↓
Prompt boundary
   ↓
Model provider
   ↓
Structured validation
   ↓
Application
```

Application secrets and provider credentials remain server-side.

The frontend interacts with MarketLane APIs rather than directly exposing privileged model credentials.

# System Design Principles

Several engineering principles emerged while building MarketLane.

### 1. Deterministic before probabilistic

If conventional software can compute something reliably, don't ask an LLM to compute it.

### 2. Context quality > bigger prompts

Better structured context usually matters more than blindly sending more tokens.

### 3. AI should be a component, not the architecture

The product needs to remain functional and understandable outside of the model itself.

### 4. Separate computation from interpretation

Analytics determine what happened.

AI helps explain why it may matter.

### 5. Build around reusable intelligence

A signal should be capable of powering dashboards, alerts, research, and AI analysis rather than being implemented repeatedly.

### 6. Treat AI output as application data

Schema, validation, versioning, evaluation, and observability matter.
